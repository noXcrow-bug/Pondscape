Built on what Windsor‑Essex already grows

Windsor Pondscape specializes in one thing: backyard ponds that recreate the local wetland ecosystem, native plants and integrated bog filtration included.

Why we specialize in native wetland ponds

Most backyard ponds are built the same way regardless of where they sit — a hole, a liner, a pump, and a few plants picked up wherever they were in stock. We took a different starting point: Windsor-Essex already sits inside one of the most distinctive ecosystems in Canada, the Carolinian zone, visible in the marshes of Point Pelee and along the Detroit River shoreline. A pond here can either work against that, or work with it.

We work with it. Every pond we build pairs a planted marginal shelf with an integrated bog filtration cell, using species native to southwestern Ontario — cardinal flower, blue flag iris, great blue lobelia, and native sedges among them. The plants aren't decoration; their root systems are doing real filtration work, the same way a natural marsh processes water. That means less chemical dependence, faster establishment, and a pond that looks like it belongs in an Essex County yard rather than a catalogue photo from somewhere else.

How we approach every project

We treat the pond, the planting plan, and the bog filtration cell as one system from the first site visit, not three separate line items. That's why design, native plant landscaping, bog filtration, maintenance, and seasonal opening and closing are all handled in-house — each stage is built with the next one in mind.
