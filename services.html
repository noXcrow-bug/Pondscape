Pond & bog filtration services for Windsor‑Essex

Five services, built to work as one connected system — from first excavation to the plant division we do five years later.

01 — Pond Design & Installation

Pond design & installation

We start with a site visit — sun exposure, drainage, soil, and how you actually plan to use the space, whether that's a quiet reflection pond or something built for koi. From there we design the basin, marginal shelf, and bog cell as one connected system, sized correctly the first time.

Installation covers excavation, underlayment and liner, plumbing for circulation through the bog cell, and any hardscaping — edging stone, viewing decks, or a small waterfall — that ties the pond into the rest of the yard. Every basin is shaped and sized for Windsor-Essex's heavy clay soils and freeze-thaw winters, not a generic template.

What's included Site assessment · basin excavation · EPDM liner & underlayment · circulation plumbing · edging & hardscape integration · initial water fill and balance

02 — Native Plant Landscaping

Native plant landscaping

Every plant we install is native to southwestern Ontario's Carolinian zone — the same climate band that makes Point Pelee one of the most biodiverse pockets in the country. That means blue flag iris, cardinal flower, great blue lobelia, native sedges, and other marginal and aquatic species chosen for their root structure as much as their bloom.

A planting plan is organized by zone — deep water, shallow marginal shelf, bog cell, and the damp soil at the pond's edge — so each species is doing a specific job, not just filling space. The result establishes faster than imported nursery stock and supports local pollinators, dragonflies, and songbirds through the season.

Typical plant palette Blue flag iris · cardinal flower · great blue lobelia · native sedges & rushes · arrowhead · swamp milkweed, matched to each pond's zones

03 — Bog Filtration Systems

Integrated bog filtration systems

This is the piece that sets our ponds apart. A bog filtration cell is a shallow, gravel-filled bed, plumbed so pond water is continuously pulled through it. As water moves through the gravel and the dense root systems of native wetland plants, nutrients and suspended solids are processed biologically — the same mechanism that keeps a natural marsh clear.

We size and layer the gravel media, plan the plumbing so flow rate matches pond volume, and select bog plants for root density as much as appearance. Done right, a bog cell reduces algae pressure and the amount of mechanical filtration and chemical dosing your pond needs long-term.

Can retrofit existing ponds Already have a pond that's fighting algae or relies on a filter box you don't love? A bog cell can often be added to an existing system rather than starting over.

04 — Pond Maintenance

Pond maintenance

Ongoing care keeps the biology of the system doing its job. That includes seasonal cleanouts, dividing and thinning plants as they establish, checking that the bog cell isn't clogging, water testing, and general upkeep of pumps and plumbing.

Because the pond is designed to filter itself biologically, maintenance visits tend to be about supporting that system rather than fighting it — a very different rhythm than a chemically-dependent pond that needs constant dosing.

What a visit covers Debris & sediment removal · plant division & thinning · bog cell inspection · pump & plumbing check · water testing

05 — Seasonal Opening & Closing

Seasonal opening & closing

Windsor-Essex winters are cold enough to matter, but short enough that a well-managed native pond can carry itself through most of the shoulder season. In fall, we prepare plants, plumbing, and any fish for the cold — protecting pumps and lines from freeze damage and cutting back growth at the right time.

In spring, we bring the system back online: checking the bog cell, restarting circulation, and dividing or replanting anything that needs it before the growing season picks up.

Timed to our climate Closing typically runs through October and November; opening runs April into May, adjusted to that year's weather rather than a fixed calendar date.
